1. What the app does, and the problem it solves

Spryte runs software for the 3DO Interactive Multiplayer, the CD-ROM console
Panasonic and Goldstar sold from 1993, on iPhone and iPad. A "spryte" is that
machine's own term for the primitive its graphics hardware draws: not a
rectangle blitted to the screen but a source image mapped corner by corner onto
an arbitrary quadrilateral, which is how its games scaled and rotated sprites
without a 3D pipeline. Implementing that correctly is the bulk of the work, and
it is where the app's name comes from.

It is not a wrapper around someone else's emulator. It emulates the machine:
the ARM60 CPU, the MADAM and CLIO chipsets, the video display list, DSP audio,
the XBUS and PBUS peripheral buses, the SPORT port and the CD-ROM subsystem.
It is built for a phone specifically: each instruction is decoded once and
cached rather than re-decoded on every execution, and frames are never
presented from inside the emulation thread.

2. The intended user and target audience

People who owned a 3DO or own its discs and want to play them on a device they
already carry, and people who want an emulator of this machine that was written
for a modern phone rather than carried forward from a desktop one.

3. The market gap, and why other apps do not fill it

The widely used 3DO cores descend from a 1990s desktop emulator. Their CPU
re-decodes every instruction on every execution, their graphics chip is scalar
C, and they present frames from inside the emulation thread, so a slow present
stalls emulation and the machine appears throttled rather than slow.

This implementation differs in kind rather than in packaging: decoded-and-cached
instructions, no present on the emulation path, and accuracy measured against a
reference implementation rather than asserted — two commercial titles run
pixel-identical to it at frame 20,000, and the build is gated on 250+
regression checks. Every chip is CRC-checked before a game starts.

5. Whether it is standalone or part of a suite on this account

Standalone. It is not a feature of another app and does not require one. The
account publishes other emulators, each for a different machine and each from a
different codebase: DOSDeck, Dad's Wire Drawer, Retro-Amiga, Retro-Saturn,
Retro-Spectrum, ST Desk, SplatLand, Stunt Car Racing 2026 and Virtua Cop
Trilogy.

6. Whether this could be an in-app purchase or content addition to one of them

No, and there is no candidate. This is a whole emulator for one machine --
processor, chipsets, graphics, audio, CD subsystem, its own implementation and
its own tests. There is no network feature, no advertising, no in-app purchase
and no account anywhere in it, so there is nothing for it to be sold inside.

7. Code or assets shared with the other apps on this account

None beyond standard third-party frameworks. The libraries are libchdr, miniz,
the LZMA SDK and zstd, each under its own licence and credited.

The interface is not shared. This app's front end is written in SwiftUI for iOS
alone, and the cross-platform interface the account's other apps used is not
compiled into it. The icon is drawn for this app and shares no element with the
others; the script that generated a single icon composition for every app does
not write it.

8. A shared codebase or content library with any third party

No code or assets are shared with any other app on the App Store. The emulator
is this developer's own implementation, written against published hardware
documentation -- the 3DO SDK headers and Graphics Programming Guide, the
granted patents covering the machine's architecture, and ARM's own ARM6
documentation -- and covered by the project's tests. No FreeDO or Opera source
is compiled into the app or vendored in its repository.

We are happy to answer any specific question about the provenance of any part
of it.

9. Whether it was created for a client, partner or other third party

No. It is Crown Park Computing's own product, designed and published by us. Its
third-party material is open-source software used under its licences with
attribution, not content supplied by a client.
